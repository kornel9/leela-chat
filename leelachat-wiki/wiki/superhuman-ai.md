# Superhuman AI & alien concepts

**Summary**: "Superhuman AI" denotes chess networks (AlphaZero, Lc0/BT4 and their descendants) that play above the level of any human and, in doing so, develop internal representations and strategic ideas that overlap with but also extend beyond human chess knowledge. "Alien concepts" are the parts of that internal knowledge that are absent from human play yet still teachable to humans once surfaced.

**Sources**: A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess.pdf, Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf, Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-02

## What it is
A superhuman chess AI is a network trained (typically by self-play reinforcement learning) to a strength no human reaches, which it does without any handcrafted opening book, endgame tablebase, or domain heuristics (source: A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess.pdf, §Domain Knowledge / §Results). For interpreting Lc0/BT4 this matters in two ways. First, because the network learned chess from scratch, its internal representations are not guaranteed to mirror human concepts — so we have to probe what it actually encodes rather than assume it thinks like a player (source: Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, Abstract). Second, because it is stronger than any human, some of what it knows is genuinely new ("alien") chess knowledge that is nonetheless recoverable and explainable — which is exactly the value proposition of a tool that translates Leela's signals to prose (source: Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf, §6.2.1).

## In the literature
- **AlphaZero (Silver et al.)** is the founding demonstration: given only the rules, it defeated the 2016 TCEC champion Stockfish 155–6 over 1,000 games, searching only ~60,000 positions/second versus Stockfish's ~58 million, and it independently rediscovered standard human openings during self-play (source: A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess.pdf, §Results / Fig. 2; §Domain Knowledge). It established superhuman strength without human knowledge as achievable. [[alphazero-science]]
- **McGrath et al. (Acquisition of Chess Knowledge)** show the superhuman network is not opaque: 200+ human chess concepts are linearly decodable from AlphaZero's activations (e.g. Stockfish total score regressed at r² > 0.75 from layer-10 activations) despite pure self-play training, and the network's training trajectory narrowed opening entropy (4.32 → ~2.8 bits) — the opposite direction from five centuries of human play, whose first-move entropy rose from 0.33 to 1.87 bits (source: Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, §4.3.2, Fig. 2a; §5.2, Fig. 5). [[acquisition-chess-knowledge-alphazero]]
- **Schut et al. (Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap)** directly operationalize "alien concepts": AlphaZero's game representations are higher-rank than human-game representations (rank 8269 vs. 7857 at layer 19), and concepts mined from that extra space were taught to four grandmasters rated 2600–2800, all of whom improved on concept puzzles (+6 to +42 percentage points, gains uncorrelated with Elo); the GMs described the ideas as "clever", "something new", and "not natural" (source: Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf, Table 1; Table 4; §6.2.1). [[bridging-human-ai-knowledge-gap]]
- **McIlroy-Young et al. (Maia)** frame skill-aligned AI as a *distinct* goal from superhuman play: raw superhuman engines do not target any human skill level (depth-limited Stockfish 33–41% move-match with monotonically increasing curves; Leela up to 46% but essentially flat), whereas Maia, retrained on human games per rating bin, hits 46–52.9% with unimodal curves peaking at each training tier (source: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, §4.2, Fig. 1–2; §4.3, Table 1). This contrasts with Schut: where Schut bridges the gap by teaching humans the AI's ideas, Maia bridges it by making the AI imitate humans. [[maia-aligning-superhuman-ai]]
- **Ruoss et al. (Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search)** show the superhuman-style strength is not tied to MCTS: a 270M-parameter transformer trained only on Stockfish-annotated action-values reaches Lichess blitz Elo 2895 against humans with no search, outperforming AlphaZero's policy net (1620) and value net (1853) used without MCTS — and a National Master characterized its style as aggressive and enterprising, distinct from Stockfish (source: Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, Abstract; Table 1; Appendix B.5). [[grandmaster-chess-without-search]]
- **Monroe & Chalmers (Mastering Chess with a Transformer)** give the architecture closest to Lc0's BT4 and show its representation captures positional ideas search engines miss: CF-240M detected a fortress draw, assigning the only drawing move (Kg7) 92.8% policy while Stockfish evaluated the same position above +3 for over 100 moves after 11 billion nodes (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, §VI, Fig. 5 Position 1). This is concrete "alien" perception — a qualitatively different representational basis from alpha-beta search. [[mastering-chess-transformer-monroe]]
- **Sandmann et al. (Iterative Inference)** add a caution about how superhuman strength is assembled inside the network: Leela's policy combines algorithmic computation with learned heuristic priors over three phases, and correct tactical solutions discovered in middle layers are sometimes overridden by safety-oriented priors in the final layers — the value head still rates the forgotten line higher, so the network's own final selection can contradict its own assessment (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §3.2 and Fig. 4–5). [[iterative-inference-chess]]

## Relevance to Leela Chat
This concept underpins the whole project's premise across phases. Phase 3 (concept probes) inherits its target concept list and probing protocol from McGrath et al., because superhuman networks encode human-decodable concepts at specific layers rather than only the final layer (source: Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, §4.3.2). Phase 5 (feature labeling) and the novelty side of Phase 3 draw on Schut et al.'s method of separating genuinely-novel-to-Lc0 features from those already in human chess books, which decides which features are most worth naming in prose (source: Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf, §4.2.2). Maia models serve Phase 6 differential analysis as human-proxy baselines that flag where Leela's move diverges from human intuition, giving grounded signal for "why is my move wrong" handlers without the LLM inventing facts (source: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, §4.4, Fig. 6). The forgotten-puzzle contradiction from Sandmann et al. is itself a directly surfaceable, hallucination-safe extractor output: when the value head rates the second-ranked line above the top-ranked one, the translator can report the disagreement (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §3.2 and Fig. 4).
