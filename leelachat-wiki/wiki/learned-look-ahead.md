# Learned look-ahead

**Summary**: Learned look-ahead is the phenomenon where a chess neural network internally represents and causally uses the squares of future moves — planning several plies ahead within a single forward pass, without any external tree search. In Lc0/BT4 interpretation it is the mechanistic claim that the policy transformer does more than pattern-match: it encodes and moves around future-move information that is causally load-bearing for the move it outputs.

**Sources**: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking.pdf, Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-02

## What it is
Learned look-ahead means the network's weights implement a search-like computation internally: it represents where a piece will go several moves into the future and uses that representation to decide its current move, all within one forward pass and with no MCTS rollouts. This matters for interpreting Lc0/BT4 because it implies the policy network's output reflects an internal plan, not just a static evaluation — so extracting "what Leela is thinking" can in principle surface which future squares the network is reasoning about, not only the top policy move. The strongest evidence is causal (patching and ablation), not merely correlational: removing future-move information from specific squares degrades the model's preferred move (source: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, Abstract). The phenomenon is most visible in tactically complex positions and along a single principal line; whether and how it combines with cheaper heuristics across the full distribution of positions is still open (source: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, §5 (Limitations)).

## In the literature
- **Evidence of learned look-ahead** is the existence proof. On a curated set of 22.5k Lichess tactics puzzles, three converging methods on Leela's policy network (T82-768x15x24h) show the network encodes and uses future-move squares: patching the third-move target square at layer 10 drops correct-move log odds by 1.88 ± 0.04 versus at most 0.55 ± 0.01 for any other square; attention head L12H12 moves information "backward in time" from the third-move target to the first-move target (zeroing its single key→query entry cuts log odds by >1.5 in over 10% of puzzles, more than ablating all 4095 other entries in that head); and a rank-32 bilinear probe reads the two-moves-ahead target square from the layer-12 residual stream at 92 ± 1% accuracy versus 15 ± 2% for a random model (source: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, §3.1 / §3.2 / §3.3). See [[evidence-learned-look-ahead]].
- **Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior** extends those findings deeper into the future. Using the same finetuned single-board-state Leela network, it shows the 15-layer policy network encodes move-square information up to 7 moves ahead (probing accuracy peaks around layer 13 and declines for more distant moves; 7th-move accuracy stays above chance but well below 3rd/5th), confirms causally non-negligible patching effects of the 5th and 7th move squares, and finds the behavior is highly context-dependent via a square-reuse taxonomy (AAC patterns show the strongest effects, ACC the weakest). It also adds two refinements: L12H12 specializes in checkmate scenarios while L12H17 complements it in non-checkmate positions, and the model considers multiple alternative branches at once — patching one branch's squares increases preference for the other (source: Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, Abstract and §3 / Fig. 5 / Fig. 6). It notes a practical warning: linear probing detects opponent-move encoding even where activation patching shows no strong causal signal, so probe-based and patching-based extractors give complementary, not redundant, signals (source: Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, §3 and Appendix C, Fig. 15). See [[understanding-look-ahead-behavior]].
- **Iterative inference in chess** locates look-ahead in the network's depth-wise computation. Applying an extended Post-LN logit lens to the same T82 network, it finds playing strength rises in three phases with a sharp late acceleration from layer 11 (logit-lens Elo: layer 11 = 1083, layer 12 = 1337, layer 13 = 1681), and explicitly notes this final-phase onset "coincides with the emergence of look-ahead attention heads in layers 11–13 identified by Jenner et al. (2024)" (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, Table 1 / §4). It adds a mechanism for "forgotten" tactics: the cumulative across-layer solve rate exceeds the final layer's, so correct forced solutions discovered mid-network can be overridden by safety-oriented priors in the last layers (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §3.2 and Fig. 3 / Fig. 5). See [[iterative-inference-chess]].
- **Acquisition of chess knowledge in AlphaZero** provides earlier, weaker evidence of in-network look-ahead in a different architecture (a 20-block ResNet). Probe accuracy for `has_mate_threat` rises throughout the network, which the authors read as evidence AlphaZero "implements at least single-move look-ahead within the network weights rather than deferring entirely to MCTS"; NMF analysis shows multi-move computations (including opponent moves) developing progressively across early convolutional blocks (source: Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, §4.3.2, Fig. 2f / §7.1.2). This is a more modest claim (single-ply within the net) than Jenner et al.'s multi-ply causal look-ahead, and is on a ResNet rather than a transformer. See [[acquisition-chess-knowledge-alphazero]].
- **Chess as a testbed for state tracking** is a useful contrast case: a from-scratch GPT2 trained on UCI move sequences learns to track board state to near-perfection (100% legal-move accuracy on starting-square prediction, 97.7% on ending-square, with 200K games) but the paper frames this as implicit world-state tracking, not lookahead, and shows full attention over the whole game history is essential — windowing to 50 tokens drops ending-square accuracy from 92.9% to 86.0% (source: Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking.pdf, Table 5 / Table 7). It demonstrates that strong implicit board modeling can exist without an established planning circuit, underscoring that look-ahead is a stronger, separate claim. See [[chess-testbed-lm-state-tracking]].
- **Grandmaster-level chess without search** sets the capability ceiling for the "no search" framing: a 270M-parameter feed-forward transformer trained on Stockfish action-values reaches Lichess blitz Elo 2895 against humans with no MCTS and no explicit search at all (source: Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, Abstract). It does not perform mechanistic interpretability and makes no look-ahead claim — but it proves grandmaster-level play is reachable from a single forward pass, which is the premise that makes "does the forward pass itself plan?" a meaningful question (source: Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, Notes). See [[grandmaster-chess-without-search]].

## Relevance to Leela Chat
Learned look-ahead maps mainly to Phase 6 (differential/counterfactual analysis) and Phase 7 (reasoning pathways): the identified backward-in-time heads (L12H12, L12H17) and the third/fifth/seventh-move target squares are concrete mechanistic components an extractor can patch or probe and a translator can narrate as steps in a plan, rather than the validator-fragile prose of "Leela is calculating" (source: Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, §3). Across the pipeline this argues for two distinct extractors — a patching-based "which future squares are causally load-bearing" extractor and a probe-based "which future squares are encoded" extractor — because the literature shows they disagree and both are needed (source: Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, §3 and Appendix C). It also gives Phase 1/handler logic a grounded, hallucination-safe signal to surface: when the network discovers a forced solution mid-depth but the final layer prefers a safer move, that policy/value contradiction can be flagged directly (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §3.2 and Fig. 4). Caution before adopting any of this: every causal result here is on the older T82 network, not the pinned BT4, and smolgen complicates the QK-derived bilinear-probe design, so the heads and layer indices must be re-verified on BT4 first (source: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, Appendix A / §3.3).
