# Maia & human-alignment

**Summary**: Human-alignment is the research direction of getting chess networks (built on the Lc0/AlphaZero stack) to predict, model, or transfer *human* play and human-relevant skill rather than only optimal play; Maia is the canonical family of AlphaZero-derived models fine-tuned on human Lichess games to match moves at a target rating.

**Sources**: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, UNDERSTANDING SKILL ADAPTATION IN TRANSFORMERS USING SPARSE AUTOENCODERS.pdf, Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-02

---

## What it is
Human-alignment treats "predict what a human at a given skill level would do" as a distinct objective from "play the strongest move." Maia repurposes the AlphaZero/Leela architecture by training the policy head on human games binned by rating, dropping tree search, so the network's raw move distribution mirrors a specific Elo band rather than engine-optimal play (source: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, §4.3, Table 1, Fig. 4). This matters for interpreting Lc0/BT4 because human-aligned models are a complementary baseline: they expose where engine intuition diverges from human intuition, give skill-stratified move labels, and provide a target for "what concepts can be taught back to humans" — all using the same 8×8×112 / 1858-move encoding Lc0 uses (source: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, §7.1, Table 3, Fig. 10).

## In the literature
- **Maia (McIlroy-Young et al., 2020)** builds nine separate models on Leela's architecture (6 residual blocks, 64 channels, SE blocks), each trained on ~12M Lichess games for a rating bin (1100–1900) with the prior 12 ply as input planes and no MCTS at inference. Maia reaches 46–52.9% move-match accuracy, strictly dominating depth-limited Stockfish (33–41%) and ordinal-rating Leela (up to 46%) on every tier; uniquely its prediction curve is *unimodal*, peaking near the training bin, whereas Stockfish curves rise monotonically and Leela curves are nearly flat (source: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, §4.3, Table 1, Fig. 4). Removing MCTS adds 5–10 pp and adding game history ~3 pp to prediction accuracy (source: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, §4.3, Fig. 5). A separate residual-CNN blunder predictor (blunder = win-probability drop ≥10 pp) hits 67.7% on board state alone and 71.7% with player metadata (source: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, §5.1, Table 2). See [[maia-aligning-superhuman-ai]].
- **ChessGPT (Feng et al., 2023)** is a language-side attempt at human-aligned chess: it uses the same R8×8×112 / R1858 encoding as Lc0/AlphaZero (source: ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, §4.1) and tries Elo-conditioned move scoring. In contrast to Maia's sharp per-bin targeting, ChessGPT *fails* to adapt to skill — scores barely move across Elo bins (700–1000: 59.4; 1700–2000: 59.6; 2700–3000: 59.8), indicating it learned a roughly average policy rather than a skill-conditioned one (source: ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, Table 8). This is a direct disagreement with Maia on whether a single model can be skill-conditioned: Maia trains separate models per bin and succeeds; ChessGPT conditions one model and does not. See [[chessgpt]].
- **Skill-adaptation SAEs (Anonymous, 2024)** opens the black box of human-alignment by training Sparse Autoencoders on the residual stream of Maia-2 (a *single* skill-conditioned model). It reports that Maia-2 treats 27% of positions monotonically (suboptimal→optimal as skill rises) versus only 1% in non-skill-aware Maia-1, locating where skill information lives (source: UNDERSTANDING SKILL ADAPTATION IN TRANSFORMERS USING SPARSE AUTOENCODERS.pdf, §5.2.1). The 2048-dim SAE reaches CC-AUC 0.88–0.90 on square-level threat concepts, and additively injecting the best threat-response feature raises best-move accuracy from 0% to over 50% at skill bracket 8 in transitional positions (random-feature interventions stay near 0%), demonstrating that skill is concentrated in identifiable, causally manipulable features (source: UNDERSTANDING SKILL ADAPTATION IN TRANSFORMERS USING SPARSE AUTOENCODERS.pdf, §5.2.2, Fig. 4 left). See [[skill-adaptation-saes]].
- **Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap (Schut et al., 2023)** works the other direction of alignment — transferring AI knowledge *to* humans. It discovers sparse concept vectors in AlphaZero's latent space (filtered for teachability and novelty), then runs a study where four grandmasters rated 2600–2800 all improved on concept puzzles (+42, +25, +16, +6 pp), with improvement uncorrelated to Elo (source: Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf, Table 4 / §6.1). Evidence that AZ encodes concepts absent from human games comes from higher-rank latent matrices for AZ vs. human play (e.g. layer 19: AZ rank 8269 vs. human 7857) (source: Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf, Table 1). See [[bridging-human-ai-knowledge-gap]].

## Relevance to Leela Chat
This concept maps primarily to Phase 6 (differential/counterfactual): Maia models act as human-proxy baselines so a `why_is_my_move_wrong` handler can flag where Lc0's move diverges sharply from human intuition without the LLM inventing chess facts (source: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, project_relevance). In Phases 2–3 the Maia skill-stratified Lichess dataset is a clean source of rating-banded human move labels for concept-probe training, and the blunder-prediction CNN (board + player metadata → win-prob-drop) is a ready template for an extractor that flags positions objectively hard for humans at the user's rating (source: Aligning Superhuman AI with Human Behavior Chess as a Model System.pdf, project_relevance). The skill-adaptation SAE methodology (CC-AUC grid search; additive single-layer feature injection) transfers to Phase 4 SAE work and Phase 6 counterfactual handlers, with the caveat that Lc0's policy head is not a linear projection from the residual stream (Smolgen attention), so the same ceiling may apply (source: UNDERSTANDING SKILL ADAPTATION IN TRANSFORMERS USING SPARSE AUTOENCODERS.pdf, project_relevance). The ChessGPT Elo-conditioning failure is a cautionary data point for any future Leela Chat personalisation feature (source: ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, project_relevance).
