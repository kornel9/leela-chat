# Linear probes & concept probing

**Summary**: A linear probe is a small linear classifier or regressor trained on a frozen network's intermediate activations to test whether a human-defined concept (material, king safety, a piece on a square) is linearly decodable from that layer; concept probing is the broader practice of using such probes to map what a chess network like Lc0/BT4 represents and where.

**Sources**: Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf, Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, Evaluation Beyond Task Performance AlphaZero in HEX.pdf, Measuring Progress in Dictionary Learning for Language Model Interpretability with Board Game Models.pdf, Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-02

## What it is
A linear probe is a lightweight linear model (logistic regression for binary concepts, linear regression for continuous ones, often L1-regularised for sparsity) trained to predict a concept label from a network's activations at a chosen layer. If the probe succeeds, the concept is "linearly available" there; the probe's accuracy/AUC and the layer at which it peaks tell you what the network represents and where it does so. This matters for interpreting Lc0/BT4 because it is the cheapest, most direct way to confirm that a chess idea (a threat, a passed pawn, the board state itself) is actually encoded in the residual stream — turning a chess fact into a number the network produced, rather than something the LLM invents. A standing caveat across the literature is that a probe only measures *correlation* (is the concept decodable?), not *causation* (does the network use it?), so probes are typically paired with interventions or behavioural tests.

## In the literature
- **AlphaZero concept acquisition** establishes the methodological template: sparse L1-regularised linear probes are trained from every layer and every training checkpoint to 200+ human chess concepts (defined via Stockfish 8's evaluation plus 116 custom features), producing "what-when-where" maps. Many concepts are recoverable with r² > 0.75 from layer-10 activations at 64k+ steps, and crucially some concepts (threats, imbalance, material) *peak at mid-network layers and decline in later layers* — meaning probing only the last block misses concepts that are linearly available earlier (source: Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, §4.3.2, Fig. 2a/d/g/h). [[acquisition-chess-knowledge-alphazero]]
- **Limits of probes** runs a head-to-head between linear probes and SAE-based probes on a 12-layer chess GPT and finds linear probes on the raw residual stream win decisively: 95% board-square classification accuracy (layers 7–8) and 88.8% Legal-Move-Probability-Mass after editing, versus only ~41% LMPM for SAE-based probes — barely above the unintervened baseline (source: Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, Abstract, §4.3/§4.4). It also shows the residual stream is Markovian for chess: identical boards reached via different move orders yield strongly aligned hidden states (Cohen's d up to 7.6 at layer 12) (source: Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, §4.5/Table 6). [[limits-of-probes]]
- **Emergent world models in chess** trains per-square, per-layer linear probes (using a Mine/Yours/Empty framing) on a GPT trained from real Lichess games and recovers the full board with 99.6% per-square accuracy (16-layer model, layer 12, F1 > 0.98 on all 13 piece states), versus ~74.7% for a random-weight baseline. It extends probing beyond board state to a *latent Elo* variable (90.5% skill-classification accuracy) and confirms causality by subtracting a piece's probe vector to make the model treat it as absent, raising the legal-move rate on the modified board from 40.5% to 92% (source: Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, §3.1/§3.2/§4.2, Table 1/Table 2). [[emergent-world-models-chess]]
- **Bridging the human–AI knowledge gap** uses sparse L1-regularised convex optimisation (a probe-like construction) over AlphaZero's latent space to *discover* concept vectors absent from human games, learnable from as few as 10 labelled examples, with concept constraints holding at 0.99 for piece concepts and 1.00 for opening concepts across layers 19–23. Amplifying a discovered concept vector improves AZ's puzzle-solving, and four grandmasters (rated 2600–2800) measurably improved (+6 to +42 points) after being taught the concepts (source: Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf, §5.1.2/Fig. 6, Table 3, Table 4). [[bridging-human-ai-knowledge-gap]]
- **Evidence of learned look-ahead** applies probing directly to Leela Chess Zero's policy transformer (the older T82-768x15x24h): a rank-32 bilinear probe (analogous to a single attention head's QK computation) predicts the optimal third move's target square with 92 ± 1% accuracy at layer 12, versus 15 ± 2% for a randomly initialised model — converging with activation-patching and ablation evidence that Leela internally represents and *causally uses* future-move squares (source: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, §3.3/Fig. 8, Abstract). [[evidence-learned-look-ahead]]
- **AlphaZero in Hex** delivers the central caution: it trains linear probing classifiers per layer with shuffled-board selectivity controls and shows that *probing performance alone is insufficient* — a concept can be encoded in activations yet not be causally used, so behavioural tests are a required complement. It also finds short-term end-game concepts are best represented in the final layers and long-term planning concepts in the middle layers, a pattern it notes echoes McGrath's chess results ("in check" high, "material imbalance" low) (source: Evaluation Beyond Task Performance AlphaZero in HEX.pdf, §1/§3.4, §4.2/Fig. 4b). [[alphazero-hex-evaluation]]
- **Measuring progress in dictionary learning** uses linear probes as the *gold-standard upper bound* against which SAEs are judged: on chess board-state recovery, linear probes reach 0.98 reconstruction F1 while the best SAE reaches only 0.85, confirming SAEs do not yet capture all linearly-encoded board information (source: Measuring Progress in Dictionary Learning for Language Model Interpretability with Board Game Models.pdf, §5, Table 1). This agrees with the Limits-of-probes finding that linear probes outperform SAEs. [[measuring-progress-dictionary-learning]]
- **The tuned lens** is a probe-adjacent technique: it trains one affine translator per layer (via KL distillation against the final-layer logits rather than ground-truth labels) to decode each intermediate residual-stream state into a vocabulary distribution, fixing the brittleness of the plain logit lens. Its causal basis extraction verifies that the directions most influential for the lens are also causally influential for the full model (Spearman ρ = 0.89), and its distillation-based training is noted as a way to sidestep the label-leakage problem probes can suffer (source: Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens.pdf, §3, §4.1/Fig. 8). [[tuned-lens]]

## Relevance to Leela Chat
Linear probing is the core of **Phase 3 (Concept Probes)**: the AlphaZero concept list (material, imbalance, king safety, mobility, threats, passed pawns, forks, pins) is the directly-adoptable target set, and the >0.9 AUC quality gate per probe mirrors these papers' methodology (source: Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, project_relevance). In the pipeline, a trained probe lives in an *extractor* that hooks BT4 activations at the right layer and emits a Pydantic finding (e.g. "knight outpost probe fired, AUC 0.94"); the *handler* assembles those firings, the *translator* turns them into prose, and the *validator* checks every cited probe label against the firing list. The literature gives two hard design constraints: probe multiple layers, not just the last block, because concepts peak at different depths (source: Evaluation Beyond Task Performance AlphaZero in HEX.pdf, §4.2; Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, §4.3.2), and pair each probe with a behavioural/causal check because decodability is not use (source: Evaluation Beyond Task Performance AlphaZero in HEX.pdf, §1) — the look-ahead and emergent-world-model interventions are the prototypes for that causal validation in later **Phase 6 (counterfactual)** work. The strong probe-beats-SAE result (source: Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, Abstract) is a standing prior to lean on Phase 3 probes before betting on Phase 4 SAE features.
