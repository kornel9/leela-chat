# Board-state probing

**Summary**: Board-state probing is the technique of training (or prompting) a decoder to recover the exact chess position — which piece sits on which square — from a network's internal activations, establishing whether the model holds a genuine "world model" of the board rather than just surface move statistics. In Lc0/BT4 it is the foundation for reading per-square facts (piece locations, derived strategic concepts) out of the residual stream so they can be cited verbatim by the translator.

**Sources**: Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking.pdf, Measuring Progress in Dictionary Learning for Language Model Interpretability with Board Game Models.pdf, Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf

## What it is
Board-state probing asks: given the network's hidden activations at some layer, can we reconstruct the literal position (the piece on each of the 64 squares)? The usual instrument is a small per-square classifier — a linear probe over the residual stream — trained to predict one of ~13 piece-type states per square, often framed as "mine/yours/empty" relative to the side to move rather than absolute white/black. It matters for interpreting Lc0/BT4 because if the board is linearly decodable, then the engine demonstrably tracks the position internally, and an extractor can read those squares out as ground-truth facts to feed the translator. The probe layer and accuracy also tell us *where* in the network the position is best represented, which is the hook point the extractor should target.

## In the literature
- **Emergent world models in chess** trains per-square linear probes (512×13 softmax classifiers) on a chess-GPT residual stream and recovers the full board at up to 99.6% per-square accuracy with F1 > 0.98 for every piece type (16-layer model, peak at layer 12), versus ~74.7% accuracy for a randomly initialized model — the headline evidence that a model trained only on human PGN forms a board-state world model, and that subtracting a piece's probe direction causally erases that piece (modified-board legal-move rate 40.5% → 92%) (source: Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, Table 1 / Table 9 / §4.2, Table 2). See [[emergent-world-models-chess]].
- **Chess as a testbed for state tracking** probes via plain next-token prompting instead of trained classifiers: because UCI notation encodes the starting square, you can prompt the model with a piece's origin and read its predicted destination, hitting 100% legal-move accuracy on starting-square prediction and 97.7% on ending-square prediction at 200K games — and it warns that full attention over the whole game history is essential (windowing to 50 tokens drops ending-square accuracy from 92.9% to 86.0% on Train-M) (source: Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking.pdf, Table 5 / Table 6 / Table 7, §5.2–5.3). See [[chess-testbed-lm-state-tracking]].
- **Measuring progress in dictionary learning** turns board reconstruction into an SAE-quality metric: it defines coverage (fraction of a board-state-property catalog recovered) and board-reconstruction F1 (recover the position from active SAE features via a high-precision rule), finding trained-model SAEs reach 0.85 chess board-reconstruction F1 versus 0.01 for random-weight models — but linear probes still beat SAEs (0.98 vs 0.85), and strategic concepts vary wildly (check = 1.00, pin = 0.33, fork = 0.38) (source: Measuring Progress in Dictionary Learning for Language Model Interpretability with Board Game Models.pdf, Table 1 / Table 4, §5). See [[measuring-progress-dictionary-learning]].
- **Exploring the limits of probes** directly pits linear probes against SAE-based probes for *editing* the board and reports the same ordering as the paper above: linear probes peak at 95% classification accuracy (layers 7–8) and 88.8% legal-move-probability-mass on the target board after an intervention, while SAE-based probes manage only ~41% (barely above the no-intervention baseline) — and it confirms the residual stream respects chess's Markovian property, with same-position-different-history hidden states aligning ever more strongly in deeper layers (Cohen's d up to 7.6 at layer 12) (source: Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, Abstract / Table 4 / Table 5 / Table 6, §4.3–4.5). See [[limits-of-probes]].
- **Decoding chess mastery** does not probe piece-by-piece board state but uses Direct Logit Attribution / logit-lens to show the chess transformer's move preference is readable from intermediate layers — destination-square preference is evident as early as layer 2 and median cosine similarity with the final output hits 92% by layer 9 — and notes the embedding vector is near-perpendicular to the output, encoding source-square (piece identity) rather than destination, a useful complement to per-square probing about *where* position information lives (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, Abstract / §4.1, Fig. 3 / Fig. 5). See [[decoding-chess-mastery]].
- **Acquisition of chess knowledge in AlphaZero** is the analogous study on a self-play ResNet (not a transformer): sparse linear probes recover 200+ human concepts from intermediate activations — Stockfish total score with r² > 0.75 from layer-10 activations — and critically shows some concepts (threats, material imbalance) peak at *mid-network* layers and decline later, so probing only the final layer misses them; that lesson carries to where board-state and strategic probes should be hooked in BT4 (source: Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, §4.3.2, Fig. 2a/d/g/h). See [[acquisition-chess-knowledge-alphazero]].
- **ChessGPT** evaluates board-state tracking behaviorally rather than by internal probes: its fine-tuned LM reaches 92–99% on BigBench chess state-tracking and >85% Levenshtein similarity on UCI/PGN-to-FEN conversion (versus <10% for LLaMA baselines), and it confirms its board/action encoding is the same R8×8×112 / R1858 representation as Lc0/AlphaZero — relevant because that shared encoding is exactly what a BT4 probe operates over (source: ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, Table 1 / Table 2 / §4.1). See [[chessgpt]].

Where these agree: the board is linearly decodable from a competent chess model's activations, and **linear probes outperform SAE-based decoders for board state** — Davis et al. (88% vs 41% intervention success) and Karvonen et al. (0.98 vs 0.85 reconstruction F1) reach this conclusion independently (source: Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, Abstract; Measuring Progress in Dictionary Learning for Language Model Interpretability with Board Game Models.pdf, §5). Caveat for Leela Chat: every one of these papers studies a GPT-style LM or a ResNet, not BT4, so the *methodology* transfers but the specific peak-accuracy layers do not.

## Relevance to Leela Chat
Board-state probing maps primarily to Phase 3 (linear concept probes), with the per-square, per-layer linear-probe protocol from Emergent World Models and the concept list from the AlphaZero study as the direct templates for the `board_state` and related extractors (source: Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, §3.1; Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, §4.3.2). The extractor must expose layer-selectable hooks rather than reading only BT4's final block, because both the AlphaZero work (mid-network concept peaks) and the chess logit-lens work (move commitment by layer 9–12) show the right layer is position- and concept-dependent; a per-layer sweep on BT4 is required since the cited layer indices come from smaller models and will not transfer. The linear-probe-beats-SAE finding is a strong prior to invest in Phase 3 probes before leaning on Phase 4 SAE features as decode or write targets, and the LMPM/board-reconstruction metrics are concrete templates for a grounding-quality signal the validator can use to confirm that every piece the translator names is actually on the board the probe reports.
