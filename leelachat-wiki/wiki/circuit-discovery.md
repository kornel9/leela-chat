# Circuit discovery

**Summary**: Circuit discovery is the process of isolating the minimal subgraph of a neural network — specific attention heads, MLPs, or sparse features and the directed edges between them — that is causally responsible for a given behavior, such as why Lc0/BT4 prefers one chess move over another.

**Sources**: Towards Automated Circuit Discovery.pdf, Sparse Feature Circuits.pdf, Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching.pdf, How to Use and Interpret Activation Patching.pdf, The Hydra Effect.pdf, Tracing the Thought of a Grandmaster-level Chess-Playing Transformer.pdf, Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-02

---

## What it is
Circuit discovery treats a transformer as a directed graph (the computational DAG of residual-stream, attention-head, MLP, or SAE-feature nodes) and asks which subgraph actually drives a target behavior. The standard workflow has three steps: pick a behavior, metric, and dataset; choose the granularity of the graph; then patch activations to prune away nodes/edges that do not matter (source: Towards Automated Circuit Discovery.pdf, Abstract). For interpreting Lc0/BT4 it matters because it is the only way to move from "this feature fires here" to "this feature is what makes Leela play Qxh7+," giving every chess claim a causal — not merely correlational — backing.

## In the literature
- **Towards Automated Circuit Discovery** automates step three of the workflow with ACDC, a greedy algorithm that walks the DAG in reverse topological order and prunes any edge whose removal raises KL divergence below a threshold; on GPT-2 Small's Greater-Than circuit it rediscovered all 5/5 component types and kept only 68 of 32,000 edges with zero false positives (source: Towards Automated Circuit Discovery.pdf, Abstract). It warns the method is highly sensitive to the corrupted-input distribution and systematically misses "negative" components that suppress wrong answers (source: Towards Automated Circuit Discovery.pdf, §4.1). [[automated-circuit-discovery]]
- **Sparse Feature Circuits** replaces coarse polysemantic nodes (heads, neurons) with fine-grained SAE features and scores each node/edge via attribution patching, yielding circuits that are both faithful and far smaller: ~100 feature nodes vs ~1500 neurons explain the same performance on Pythia-70M, ~500 vs ~50,000 on Gemma-2-2B (source: Sparse Feature Circuits.pdf, Fig. 3). It keeps SAE error terms as first-class nodes rather than discarding unexplained variance (source: Sparse Feature Circuits.pdf, §2). [[sparse-feature-circuits]]
- **Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching** generalizes node-level discovery to *path*-level: it localizes behavior to directed interaction paths and scores hypotheses with the Average Unexplained Effect (AUE), where AUE=0 means the hypothesis fully explains the behavior on the tested distribution (source: Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching.pdf, §2.3, Eq. 1). Iterative refinement of an induction-head hypothesis raised the proportion explained from 28.2% to 73.5% (source: Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching.pdf, §3.5, Table 3). It cautions that AUE measures sufficiency, not completeness (source: Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching.pdf, §7.1). [[path-patching]]
- **How to Use and Interpret Activation Patching** is the methods guide underneath circuit discovery, framing denoising (clean→corrupt) as finding components *sufficient* to restore behavior and noising (corrupt→clean) as finding components *necessary* to maintain it, and recommending continuous logit-difference metrics over probability for exploratory patching (source: How to Use and Interpret Activation Patching.pdf, §3, §4.1). It flags that negative components can make a circuit look complete when only half the positive components are found (source: How to Use and Interpret Activation Patching.pdf, §3.2). [[activation-patching-guide]]
- **The Hydra Effect** is the central caveat for any circuit-discovery effort: ablating one attention layer in a 7B model causes a downstream layer to compensate, collectively restoring ~70% of the lost logit signal, so total-effect and direct-effect importance are substantially uncorrelated for most layers (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, §6 Conclusion; §2.5 / Figure 2c). The authors note this self-repair specifically challenges ACDC-style automated discovery, because total-effect priority ordering no longer reflects the intact network's structure (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, §6 Conclusion). [[hydra-effect]]
- **Tracing the Thought of a Grandmaster-level Chess-Playing Transformer** is the chess-domain instantiation: it decomposes both BT4's MLPs (Transcoders) and attention (Lorsa) and builds "reasoning pathways" — full-model circuit graphs whose nodes are sparse features and whose edges are measured causal feature-to-feature effects, constructed via feature steering (source: Tracing the Thought of a Grandmaster-level Chess-Playing Transformer.pdf, §3, Algorithm 1). Concrete causal numbers anchor the circuit: ablating the d3->h7 bishop-control attention dropped P(Qxh7+) from 28.75% to 23.35%, and ablating an own-pawn-detection feature flipped it from 28.8% to ~91% (source: Tracing the Thought of a Grandmaster-level Chess-Playing Transformer.pdf, §4, Table 4). [[fudan-tracing-thought]]
- **Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior** applies circuit-discovery techniques (activation patching, probing, zero ablation) to Lc0's policy network and localizes specific components: attention head L12H12 acts as a general "backward-in-time" information mover from future-move squares to the first-move square, specializing in checkmate scenarios, while L12H17 handles non-checkmate positions (source: Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, §3, Fig. 5). It warns that probing can detect opponent-move encoding that patching misses, so probe-based and patching-based circuit signals are complementary, not redundant (source: Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, §3 and Appendix C). [[understanding-look-ahead-behavior]]
- **Decoding Chess Mastery** uses Direct Logit Attribution (a logit-lens variant) to trace vertical information flow in the LCB chess transformer, finding that by layer 9 the median cosine similarity between intermediate DLA vectors and the final move output reaches 92%, so most computation completes before the last layers; it also proposes a taxonomy of 8 structural attention-head patterns (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, §4.1 / Fig. 5; Table 2). Note this analyzes LCB (a GPT-2-small model trained on human games), not Lc0/BT4, so the layer-depth and pattern findings may not transfer quantitatively (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, notes). [[decoding-chess-mastery]]

## Relevance to Leela Chat
Circuit discovery maps primarily to Phase 6 (differential and counterfactual analysis) and Phase 7 (reasoning pathways). In Phase 6, the `why_is_my_move_wrong` handler will run path patching / activation patching over BT4's residual stream to isolate which paths carry the decisive evaluation difference between the user's move and Leela's top move, using logit difference as the exploratory metric (source: How to Use and Interpret Activation Patching.pdf, §4.1). In Phase 7, the pathway extractor reproduces the Fudan reasoning-pathway algorithm directly on the pinned BT4 network so the LLM translator can narrate the resulting feature-to-feature circuit as ordered steps (source: Tracing the Thought of a Grandmaster-level Chess-Playing Transformer.pdf, §3, Algorithm 1). The Hydra-effect caveat must be enforced across the pipeline: extractors should use multi-component ablation and the validator should never let prose over-claim circuit completeness, since both ACDC's missed-negative-components limit and path patching's sufficiency-not-completeness limit make single-component conclusions unreliable (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, §6 Conclusion; source: Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching.pdf, §7.1).
