# Attention mechanism

**Summary**: The attention mechanism is the transformer sublayer where each board-square token reads from other tokens via query-key-value computations; in Lc0/BT4 individual attention heads specialize (piece-movement, legal-destination, backward-in-time look-ahead) and are the components mechanistic interpretability ablates, patches, and prunes to localize chess reasoning.

**Sources**: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching.pdf, Towards Automated Circuit Discovery.pdf, Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, The Hydra Effect.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-02

---

## What it is
Attention is the transformer operation that lets each token (in chess networks, one token per board square) selectively read information from every other token using learned query, key, and value projections, then write the weighted result into the [[residual-stream]]. It matters for interpreting Lc0/BT4 because attention is where spatial relationships between squares are mixed: many heads turn out to encode specific chess semantics (which piece can reach a square, where a move's consequences land), and a single head can carry an outsized share of a tactical decision. Attention heads are therefore the natural unit of analysis for the mechanistic-interpretability toolkit — they are what activation patching swaps, what circuit-discovery algorithms prune, and what ablation studies knock out. A key BT4-specific caveat: Lc0's smolgen module modifies attention scores before the softmax, so its attention is not vanilla transformer attention (source: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, Appendix A).

## In the literature
- **Mastering chess with a transformer (Chessformer/CF-240M, the architecture BT4 follows)** uses the Shaw et al. relative-position scheme inside self-attention — learnable per-pair query/key/value vectors of full embedding depth — which beats absolute position embeddings by 1.83% policy accuracy, more than doubling model size gives (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, §IV-C, Table I). Attention-map analysis finds most heads specialize in one piece type's movement (bishop, knight, rook, king) and stay specialized even when that piece is off the board; a layer-11 head (head 2) attends to pieces that can move to the querying square but appears only in the second half of the network, implying it consumes movement facts computed earlier; and chess tokens rarely attend to themselves, unlike language models (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, §V). See [[mastering-chess-transformer-monroe]] and [[transformer-chess-architecture]].
- **Evidence of learned look-ahead** isolates head L12H12 (layer 12, head 12) as a "backward-in-time" mover: in 29.8% of puzzles its largest attention entry runs from the first-move target (query) to the third-move target (key), and zeroing that single entry drops correct-move log odds by more than 1.5 in over 10% of puzzles — a larger effect than ablating all 4095 other entries in the same head combined (source: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, §3.2, Fig. 5). It also shows piece-movement heads propagate consequence information forward: ablating their key-outflow from the third-move target square cuts log odds by ≥1.5 in 60% of puzzles (source: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, §3.2, Fig. 7). See [[evidence-learned-look-ahead]] and [[learned-look-ahead]].
- **Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior** extends this to the 5th and 7th future moves and refines the head story: L12H12 is a general, timing-insensitive backward-mover that responds far more strongly in checkmate scenarios, while L12H17 takes over in non-checkmate positions — distinct heads for distinct tactical situations (source: Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, §3, Fig. 5). It argues these are general learned mechanisms, not memorized rules, since identical square-reuse patterns produce consistent head effects regardless of board location (source: Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior.pdf, §3). See [[understanding-look-ahead-behavior]].
- **Decoding chess mastery** (on the GPT-2-small LCB model, not Lc0) catalogs the *shapes* attention takes: averaging over 1,000 games it identifies 8 structural attention-pattern types (Checkers, Big Checkers, Window Pane, Columns, Waffle, Backslash, Amorphous, Polysemantic), with Big Checkers most common at 20 of 144 heads, and "Amorphous" diffuse heads occurring only in the final layer (L12 H1–H3) (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, §4, Table 2). Caveat: LCB is human-game-trained without smolgen, so these frequencies should not be assumed to transfer to BT4 (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, Notes). See [[decoding-chess-mastery]].
- **Iterative inference in chess** independently times when these heads matter: Leela's sharp late-layer strengthening begins at layer 11, coinciding with the emergence of the look-ahead attention heads in layers 11–13 identified by Jenner et al. (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §4). This pins the look-ahead attention machinery to the network's final computational phase. See [[iterative-inference-chess]].
- **Localizing model behavior with path patching** treats attention heads as nodes and the directed interactions *between* them as paths, arguing path-level localization is strictly more expressive than node-level ablation; on the GPT-2 number task it implicates fewer attention heads than causal tracing while staying more on-distribution, and a greedy search over just 8 of 144 heads recovers 98.0% of the loss (source: Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching.pdf, §1/§4, Fig. 11). See [[path-patching]].
- **Towards automated circuit discovery (ACDC)** automates pruning the attention-head/MLP edge graph to a sparse responsible subgraph — on GPT-2's Greater-Than circuit it kept 68 of 32,000 edges and rediscovered all 5/5 component types with zero false positives — but systematically *misses* "negative" attention heads that actively suppress the correct answer, because its greedy KL objective never rewards including them (source: Towards Automated Circuit Discovery.pdf, Abstract / §4.1). See [[automated-circuit-discovery]] and [[circuit-discovery]].
- **The Hydra effect** is the warning that ties the ablation methods together: in a 7B language model, ablating one attention layer causes a downstream attention layer to increase its direct effect and compensate, explaining up to 92% of the variance in downstream direct-effect changes (layer 23 of 32) and collectively restoring about 70% of the lost logit signal (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, §4.2/§6, Fig. 7b). Consequently ablation-based and direct-effect importance are largely uncorrelated, so a low single-head ablation impact does not mean the head is unimportant — and this poses a direct challenge for ACDC-style discovery (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, §2.5/§6, Fig. 2c). The chess relevance is asserted by analogy (same research group), not yet verified on BT4 (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, Notes). See [[hydra-effect]] and [[self-repair]].

## Relevance to Leela Chat
Attention heads are the mechanistic units Leela Chat surfaces in Phase 6 (differential/counterfactual) and Phase 7 (reasoning pathways): heads like L12H12/L12H17 are concrete, named components an extractor can report and the translator can narrate as steps in a look-ahead, and the piece-movement/legal-destination head specializations (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, §V) constrain where to hook the network — early layers for local movement facts, layers 11–13 for look-ahead synthesis (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §4). Path patching and ACDC (source: Localizing Model Behavior with Path Patching.pdf, §1; source: Towards Automated Circuit Discovery.pdf, Abstract) are the tooling for isolating which heads drive a position's evaluation, but the Hydra effect (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, §6) must feed validator/handler discipline so prose never over-claims that a single ablated head was "the" cause. Two BT4-specific cautions carry into every head-level extractor: smolgen makes attention non-vanilla (source: Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.pdf, Appendix A), and the policy head is itself a 64×64 self-attention traversal rather than a linear read-off, so policy logits cannot be logit-lensed off the residual stream (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, Appendix A-C).
