# World models (internal board state)

**Summary**: A "world model" is an internal, recoverable representation of the actual chess position — which piece sits on which square — that a network builds in its activations even when it is only trained to predict moves or values; for interpreting Lc0/BT4 it is the foundational claim that the network "knows where the pieces are" rather than pattern-matching surface move strings.

**Sources**: Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking.pdf, Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-02

---

## What it is
A world model in a chess network is an internal encoding of the board state — the location and identity of every piece — that can be read out of intermediate activations, even though the network was never explicitly given a board representation and was trained only on move sequences, action-values, or self-play outcomes. The standard test is to train a linear probe on the residual stream and ask whether it can reconstruct the position; high per-square accuracy is taken as evidence the network maintains a real internal board rather than memorising surface statistics. This matters for interpreting Lc0/BT4 because the entire Leela Chat premise — that extracted facts trace back to numbers the network produced — rests on the network actually representing the position it is reasoning about, and the literature shows that representation can be probed, edited, and causally validated.

## In the literature
- **Emergent world models in chess** is the strongest direct demonstration: a GPT trained only on next-character prediction over 16M real Lichess games (no a priori chess knowledge) develops a linear board-state representation that linear probes recover with **99.6% per-square accuracy** (16-layer model, layer 12; F1 > 0.98 on all 13 piece-type states) versus ~74.7% for a random-weight baseline. It confirms the representation is causal, not merely correlated: subtracting a piece's probe vector from the residual stream makes the model treat that piece as absent, raising the legal-move rate on the modified board from 40.5% to 92%. It also extends world-model probing to a latent Elo variable (90.5% skill-classification accuracy), and notably contradicts the earlier belief that world models only emerge from *synthetic* data (source: Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, §3.1/§4.2, Table 1/Table 2). [[emergent-world-models-chess]]
- **Chess as a testbed for state tracking** proposes chess as a controlled probe of transformer world-state tracking and shows a GPT2 trained on UCI move sequences reaches **100% legal-move accuracy on starting-square prediction** with 200K games, and 97.7% on ending squares — all without any explicit board representation. Its central caveat is that this world model depends on access to full history: windowing attention to 50 tokens drops ending-square accuracy from 92.9% to 86.0% (Train-M), and path-obstruction errors (which require tracking several pieces at once) remain stubborn even at full scale (source: Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking.pdf, Table 5/Table 7, Appendix F). [[chess-testbed-lm-state-tracking]]
- **Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero** extends the world-model idea from raw board squares to *human chess concepts*: sparse linear probes recover 200+ concepts (material, king safety, threats, mobility) from AlphaZero's self-play-trained ResNet, with Stockfish total score regressed at **r² > 0.75 from layer-10 activations** at 64k+ steps — striking because AlphaZero never saw human games. A key world-model finding is that the representation is *layer-localised*: some concepts (threats, imbalance, material) peak at mid-network layers and decline later, so the internal board/concept state is not concentrated in one final layer (source: Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, §4.3.2, Fig. 2a/d/g/h). [[acquisition-chess-knowledge-alphazero]]
- **Limits of probes** stress-tests the world model by trying to *edit* it, and surfaces a property directly useful to Leela Chat: the chess GPT's residual stream is **Markovian** — identical board positions reached via different move orders produce strongly aligned hidden states (Cohen's d rising to 7.6 at layer 12), confirming the network encodes the *position itself*, not the path to it. On editing that world model, linear probes on the raw residual stream achieve 88% Legal-Move-Probability-Mass on the target board versus only ~41% for SAE-based probes (source: Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, §4.5/Table 6, Abstract). This Markovian result agrees with and reinforces the emergent-world-models claim that the internal board is a coherent state object. [[limits-of-probes]]
- **ChessGPT** treats world-modelling as one of three benchmark axes ("chess modeling ability / state tracking"): a fine-tuned RedPajama-3B reaches **92–99% on BigBench chess state tracking** and >85% Levenshtein similarity on UCI/PGN-to-FEN conversion, versus 23–53% and <10% for LLaMA/RedPajama baselines. Crucially it uses the *same R8×8×112 board encoding and R1858 move vector as Lc0/AlphaZero*, so its world-model results are framed in exactly the representation Leela Chat's extractors operate on (source: ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, Table 1/Table 2, §4.1). [[chessgpt]]
- **Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search** marks the boundary of a *minimal* world model: its 270M-parameter transformer reaches Lichess blitz Elo 2895 from a **FEN-only input with almost no history**, proving a strong implicit board representation can be built from a single position snapshot — but the same minimal history makes it blind to threefold repetition (worked around by forcing the repetition move's win-percentage to 50%) and indecisive when several moves all map to the 100% value bin. This is the direct architectural analogue to Leela Chat's FEN-based position loading and a concrete warning that a position-only world model loses history-dependent facts (source: Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, §5, §2.2). [[grandmaster-chess-without-search]]
- **Bridging the human–AI knowledge gap** probes the world model from the other direction — its *richness*: AlphaZero's latent representation has a **higher rank than human-game representations** (AZ rank 8269 vs. human 7857 at layer 19; 6771 vs. 6544 at layer 23), evidence that the internal model encodes concepts absent from human play. Sparse concept vectors extracted from that space were learnable from as few as 10 examples and, when taught, measurably improved four grandmasters (+6 to +42 points) — demonstrating the world model contains transferable, novel structure beyond raw board state (source: Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.pdf, Table 1, §5.1.2/Table 4). [[bridging-human-ai-knowledge-gap]]

## Relevance to Leela Chat
The world-model claim underwrites **Phase 1 (network-first chat MVP)** and is the precondition for **Phase 3 (concept probes)**: only because BT4 maintains a recoverable internal board can a `board_state` extractor hook the residual stream and emit a Pydantic finding the translator turns into prose and the validator checks for legality. The Markovian-residual-stream and per-layer-localisation findings give two design constraints — a single board-state extraction per FEN is sufficient (the position, not the move-path, is what is encoded), but extractors must sample multiple BT4 layers because the internal board/concept state peaks at different depths, not only the final block (source: Exploring_the_Limits_of_Probes_for_Latent_Represen.pdf, §4.5; Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.pdf, §4.3.2). The vector-subtraction editing result is the prototype for **Phase 6 (counterfactual)** handlers, and the FEN-only minimal-world-model pitfalls (threefold repetition, indecisive winning positions) are concrete cases for the validator and for preserving FEN metadata per CLAUDE.md's implementation pitfalls (source: Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, §5).
