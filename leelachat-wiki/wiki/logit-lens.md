# Logit lens & tuned lens

**Summary**: The logit lens decodes a transformer's intermediate residual-stream states into the model's own output vocabulary (move/policy or value distributions) by projecting them through the unembedding/head, letting us watch when an Lc0/BT4-style network "decides" on a move layer by layer; the tuned lens is its trained, less-biased successor that learns one affine translator per layer.

**Sources**: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens.pdf, Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, How to Use and Interpret Activation Patching.pdf, The Hydra Effect.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-02

---

## What it is
The logit lens takes a residual-stream vector from an intermediate transformer layer and projects it through the model's unembedding matrix (or policy/value head) to read out a prediction distribution "as if" that layer were the last one — exposing the layer-by-layer build-up of the final decision. The tuned lens improves on this by training a small affine transform (a "translator") per layer via KL distillation against the final-layer output, correcting the systematic bias and representational drift that make the plain logit lens brittle (source: Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens.pdf, §3). For interpreting Lc0/BT4 this matters because it answers *when* in the network a move preference forms, which layer to hook for extraction, and whether intermediate "decisions" survive to the output — but it requires care because both Leela's Post-LN architecture and its smolgen attention break the naive math.

## In the literature
- **Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network** extends the logit lens to Leela's Post-LN policy network (T82-768x15x24h) by zero-ablating sublayer outputs beyond the probe point while preserving subsequent layer norms and DeepNorm α scaling (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §2.2 and Appendix B). It finds a three-phase progression in lensed playing strength — rapid early gains to layer 5 (Elo ~1007), a plateau through layer 10, then sharp strengthening (layer 12=1337, layer 13=1681, full model=2263) (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, Table 1) — with Kendall's τ between intermediate and final move rankings near zero or negative until rising sharply only around layer 12 (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §3.2 and Fig. 7). It also documents a "forgotten-puzzle" phenomenon where correct tactical solutions appear in middle layers and are overridden by safety-oriented final layers (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §3.2 and Fig. 5). See [[iterative-inference-chess]].
- **Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens** introduces the tuned lens itself, training one affine translator per layer by KL distillation against the final-layer logits to fix the logit lens's ~4–5 bits of KL bias seen across most layers of GPT-Neo-2.7B (source: Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens.pdf, Fig. 3; §3). It validates causal relevance via causal basis extraction, finding Spearman ρ=0.89 between a direction's tuned-lens influence and its causal influence on the full model (source: Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens.pdf, §4.1, Fig. 8), and notes the original SGD-trained lenses were severely undertrained relative to a Muon-optimizer retrain (source: Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens.pdf, §3, Muon optimizer subsection). See [[tuned-lens]].
- **Decoding Chess Mastery** applies the logit-lens technique under the name Direct Logit Attribution (DLA) to the GPT-2-small LCB chess transformer, projecting intermediate residual-stream vectors through the unembed matrix into move space; by layer 9 the median cosine similarity between DLA vectors and the final output reaches 92%, and the destination-square preference is evident as early as layer 2 (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, §4.1 / Fig. 5; Abstract and Fig. 3). It contributes a probability-mass-weighted Magnitude Precision@K metric that stays stable from k=1 to k=2, showing early-layer alternatives are high quality even when their top-1 rank differs (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, §4.1 / Fig. 6). Note this is a human-game-trained GPT-2-small model, not Lc0/BT4, so its layer-depth numbers may not transfer (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, notes). See [[decoding-chess-mastery]].
- **How to Use and Interpret Activation Patching** treats the logit lens (the "unembedding-based direct-effect" view) as one of the metrics in its patching toolkit and warns it can saturate: logprob saturates once the correct answer becomes top-ranked, so logit difference is recommended as the smoother exploratory metric (source: How to Use and Interpret Activation Patching.pdf, §4.2; §4.1, §5). It also flags that backup/Hydra-effect components compensate at ~0.7× of an ablated component's original boost, making ablation-based importance understate true importance by ~0.3× (source: How to Use and Interpret Activation Patching.pdf, §3.2). See [[activation-patching-guide]].
- **The Hydra Effect** formalizes the logit lens as the "unembedding-based direct effect" (with RMSNorm scale fixed) and proves it is equivalent to the causal direct effect when the ablation source is drawn from the correct distribution (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, §3.1). Its central caution for lens-based work: ablation-based (total-effect) and unembedding-based (direct-effect) importance are substantially uncorrelated for most layers, so a low logit-lens-measured ablation impact does not imply low importance (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, §2.5 / Figure 2c), and downstream compensation restores ~70% of the lost logit signal (source: The Hydra Effect.pdf, §6 Conclusion). See [[hydra-effect]].

These papers broadly agree the plain logit lens reveals real layer-wise structure but is imperfect: the tuned lens corrects its bias ([[tuned-lens]]), the chess papers show its readout is only fully reliable late in the network ([[iterative-inference-chess]], [[decoding-chess-mastery]]), and the patching/self-repair papers warn that lens-derived direct-effect importance can diverge sharply from true causal importance ([[activation-patching-guide]], [[hydra-effect]]).

## Relevance to Leela Chat
The logit/tuned lens maps primarily to Phase 1 (network-first chat) — deciding which layer of BT4 to hook for policy/value extraction, since meaningful policy signal only stabilizes in the late layers (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §3.2 and Fig. 7) — and to Phase 3 (concept probes), where the tuned lens's per-layer distillation-trained translators offer a probe-design template that sidesteps label-leakage concerns (source: Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens.pdf, project_relevance). In the pipeline an extractor would read intermediate residual-stream activations and lens them to a grounded findings number (e.g., the layer at which a move's lensed rank crosses a threshold, or a value-head vs. policy-head disagreement flagging a "forgotten" tactic per source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §3.2 and Fig. 4), which the translator then narrates as prose and the validator checks against the cited ranks. Two hard caveats apply before any BT4 lens extractor ships: the Post-LN/DeepNorm zero-ablation extension is a prerequisite (source: Iterative Inference in a Chess-Playing Neural Network.pdf, §2.2 and Appendix B), and BT4's smolgen attention means the logit-lens math differs from a vanilla transformer (source: Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens.pdf, project_relevance).
