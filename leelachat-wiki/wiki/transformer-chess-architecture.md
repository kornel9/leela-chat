# Transformer chess architecture

**Summary**: The transformer-based design of modern chess networks — encoder/decoder self-attention stacks that read a tokenized board and output move policies and value estimates — as opposed to the convolutional ResNets of the original AlphaZero. For Lc0/BT4, knowing this architecture (how squares are tokenized, how attention encodes spatial relationships, how the policy head works) is what tells you where to hook activations and which interpretability shortcuts are unsafe.

**Sources**: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess.pdf, ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking.pdf, Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf

**Last updated**: 2026-06-02

## What it is
A transformer chess network treats the board as a sequence of tokens (one per square, or one per move in notation-based models) and processes them through stacked self-attention and MLP blocks, emitting a move-probability distribution (policy) and an outcome estimate (value). This matters for interpreting Lc0/BT4 because BT4 is exactly such a transformer — not the convolutional ResNet of the original AlphaZero — so its internal "highway" is a per-square residual stream shaped by attention, and its policy head is its own attention mechanism rather than a linear readout. Understanding the tokenization, the position-encoding scheme, and the head design determines which layers carry which signals and which interpretability moves (logit lens on policy, probing a given layer) are valid versus misleading. The historical contrast is AlphaZero, whose chess network was a 19-block, 256-filter convolutional ResNet with policy and value heads (source: A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess.pdf, §Architecture).

## In the literature
- **Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model (Chessformer/CF-240M)** is the direct architecture reference for BT4: an encoder-only transformer with a 64-token context (one token per square), 15 layers, 1024 embedding depth, and 32 heads (243M params), reaching 2347 Elo and 93.5% puzzle accuracy at 30x fewer FLOPS than the prior grandmaster-level agent (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, Table II / Notes). It replaces standard position embeddings with the Shaw et al. relative-position scheme (learnable per-pair vectors in attention), which beats absolute embeddings by 1.83% policy accuracy on a 6M model — more than doubling model size gains (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, §IV-C, Table I). Critically, its policy head is itself a self-attention mechanism producing a 64×64 (from, to) traversal matrix, so policy logits are NOT a simple linear projection from the residual stream (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, Appendix A-C). See [[mastering-chess-transformer-monroe]].
- **A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess (AlphaZero)** is the convolutional predecessor, not a transformer: a 19-residual-block, 256-filter ResNet taking an 8×8×(MT+L) image stack (T=8 history steps plus castling, repetition, and no-progress planes) and emitting an 8×8×73 policy over 4,672 moves (source: A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess.pdf, §Representation / §Architecture). The MCTS training paradigm carries over to Lc0, but the architectural details do not — this is the contrast against which the transformer chess networks are defined (source: A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess.pdf, Notes via §Architecture). See [[alphazero-science]].
- **Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search** trains a 270M-parameter decoder-only transformer on FEN-tokenized board states (77-character fixed-length FEN, ~1968 legal actions indexed as tokens) to predict Stockfish action-values, reaching Lichess blitz Elo 2895 with no search (source: Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, Abstract / §2.2). It demonstrates depth saturates around 8 layers when parameters are held constant — depth matters but has diminishing returns (source: Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, §3.4, Table 2). Because it sees only the current FEN, it is blind to threefold repetition, requiring a workaround (source: Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, §5).
- **ChessGPT** fine-tunes a RedPajama-3B GPT-style causal LM on a chess corpus and trains a ChessCLIP model using the same R8×8×112 board / R1858 move encoding as Lc0 and AlphaZero (source: ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, §4.1). It reaches 92–99% on BigBench chess state tracking versus 23–53% for LLaMA/RedPajama baselines, evidence that a transformer LM internalizes board mechanics from move sequences (source: ChessGPT Bridging Policy Learning and Language Modeling.pdf, Table 1). See [[chessgpt]].
- **Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking** trains GPT2-small (12 layers, 12 heads, 768-d, 512 context) on UCI move sequences and probes board-state tracking, finding that full attention over the entire game history is essential: windowing attention to 50 tokens drops legal-move ending-square accuracy from 92.9% to 86.0%, and approximate-attention architectures (Reformer) degrade further (source: Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking.pdf, Table 7, §5.3). With 200K games it reaches 100% legal-move accuracy on starting-square prediction (source: Chess as a Testbed for Language Model State Tracking.pdf, Table 5, §5.2). See [[chess-testbed-lm-state-tracking]].
- **Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models** trains 8- and 16-layer GPT models (25M/50M params) on 16M Lichess games and shows board-state accuracy depends on depth: the 16-layer model peaks at layer 12 (99.6% per-square), the 8-layer model at layer 6 — deeper models use proportionally more layers to build a more accurate world model (source: Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, §3.1, Fig. 3 / Table 1). See [[emergent-world-models-chess]].
- **Decoding Chess Mastery** runs mechanistic analysis on the LCB GPT2-small chess transformer and finds computation largely completes before the final layers: by layer 9 the median cosine similarity between intermediate Direct-Logit-Attribution vectors and the final output reaches 92% (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, §4.1, Fig. 5). It proposes a taxonomy of 8 structural attention-head patterns (Checkers, Big Checkers, Window Pane, Columns, Waffle, Backslash, Amorphous, Polysemantic), with diffuse "Amorphous" heads appearing only in the final layer (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, §4, Table 2). See [[decoding-chess-mastery]].

Two notes of disagreement/caveat across these papers: (1) the language-model studies (Decoding Chess Mastery, Chess as a Testbed, Emergent World Models) analyze GPT-2-style human-game-trained models, NOT Lc0/BT4 — their specific layer indices and pattern frequencies are explicitly flagged as not transferring quantitatively to BT4's 15-layer self-play architecture (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, Notes; source: Emergent World Models and Latent Variable Estimation in Chess-Playing Language Models.pdf, Notes). (2) On where computation finishes: Decoding Chess Mastery reports ~92% convergence to the final output by layer 9 in LCB (source: Decoding Chess Mastery.pdf, §4.1), whereas the broader literature on Leela itself (cited in sibling pages) finds move preferences stabilize even later; the methodology transfers but the depth numbers are model-specific.

## Relevance to Leela Chat
This concept underpins Phase 1 (network-first chat MVP) and every later interpretability phase, because BT4 is the Chessformer/CF-240M architecture (15 layers, 1024 embedding, 32 heads) that all extractors hook into (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, Notes). The Shaw relative-position encoding governs what each square token's residual stream encodes about its neighbors, and the §V attention analysis (early layers accumulate piece-movement facts, late layers synthesize legal-reach and opponent-presence) constrains which layer the network-internal extractors target (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, §V). The single most load-bearing architectural fact for the pipeline is that the policy head is a 64×64 self-attention traversal, NOT a linear projection from the residual stream — so any handler or validator tempted to run a logit lens on the policy head is unsafe and must be avoided (source: Mastering Chess with a Transformer Model.pdf, Appendix A-C). The FEN-only, history-light failure modes (threefold-repetition blindness, indecision when many moves saturate the top value bin) are concrete validator test cases inherited from this architecture family (source: Grandmaster-Level Chess Without Search.pdf, §5).
