### Under "Tier 2 — Major SAE and interpretability dependencies"

#### Tang et al. — Skill-Adaptation SAEs on Maia-2 ⭐

- **Title:** *Understanding Skill Adaptation in Transformers Using Sparse Autoencoders: Chess as a Model System*
- **Status:** Under review at ICLR 2024 (anonymous submission as of the version in this folder)
- **Code:** `https://anonymous.4open.science/r/chess-sae-3C06/`
- **License:** check anonymous repo before integrating
- **Subject model:** Maia-2 (the *unified* skill-aware Maia, not the Maia-1 ensemble already in PRIOR_WORK). Maia-2 has a ResNet backbone + skill-aware transformer blocks; skill embeddings (active + opponent) are injected into the attention queries.
- **What it does:** Trains SAEs on Maia-2's *skill-modulated* residual stream representations, identifies latent features that fire on interpretable chess concepts, then performs mediated interventions on those SAE features to elicit higher- or lower-skill play in the same position.
- **Why it matters for this project:** The product's coaching aspiration (CLAUDE.md §1 "personal use → strong-player friends → maybe alpha"; BIBLE.md teaching/coaching framing) is closest to what this paper actually does. They have a concrete intervention experiment — push a feature, the model plays better or worse on the *same board* — which is the cleanest experimental template for Phase 6's `why_is_my_move_wrong` and "what would a 2400 player see here?" handlers.
- **Where it fits:**
  - **Phase 4:** if the Fudan repo go/no-go fails, this paper's pipeline is a backup architecture for *skill-aware* SAE training. The model is different (Maia-2 not Lc0 BT4) but the methodology transfers.
  - **Phase 6:** their "mediated intervention with SAE features" is, structurally, the Phase 6 counterfactual experiment. Adopt their evaluation protocol (paired-position before/after intervention metrics) as a template.
- **Caveats:** Maia-2 architecture differs from Lc0 BT4 (ResNet+transformer hybrid vs. pure transformer with smolgen). Don't expect direct feature transfer; the methodology transfers, not the weights.

### Under "Tier 3 — Mechanistic interpretability toolkits" or as a new "Reference implementations" subsection

#### alphasae (Pavan Yellow / pavanyellow)

- **Repo:** zip in this folder (`alphasae-main.zip`); origin appears to be `github.com/pavanyellow/alphasae`
- **License:** Apache 2.0 ✓ (permissive, commercial use allowed)
- **Stack:** torch + numpy + tqdm + wandb (4 dependencies total; that's not a typo)
- **What it does:** Trains a small AlphaZero on Othello 6x6 (`alphazero/` + `othello/`), captures Layer-2 residual stream activations (3M examples), then trains 25 SAEs across a 5×5 grid of L1 penalty × feature count `[1..5] × [256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096]`. Includes a static HTML feature-browser at `vis/feature_vis.html`. Self-reported training time: 1–30 min per SAE on an RTX 4090.
- **Why it's worth keeping:** *Not* a production dependency. It's a clean, four-dependency, single-author reference implementation of the entire "activations → SAE → feature browser" pipeline on a board-game AlphaZero. Two specific uses:
  - **Phase 4 pedagogical:** Read the SAE training loop in `sae/trainer.py` *before* fighting with OpenMOSS or the Fudan repo. It's small enough to fit in your head, and it makes the Fudan and OpenMOSS abstractions less mysterious.
  - **Phase 5 UX inspiration:** The static HTML feature browser (`vis/index.html`) is a working example of "click a feature, see top-activating positions" — exactly the labeling tool you'd otherwise build from scratch. Style is much simpler than Neuronpedia; closer to what your alpha would actually ship with.
- **What it does NOT do:** chess. Lc0. BT4. Transcoders. Lorsas. Reasoning pathways. It is not a Fudan substitute and should not be treated as one for the Phase 4 go/no-go.

### Under "Research — Chess interpretability (primary)"

#### Cruz — "Understanding the learned look-ahead behavior of chess neural networks" ⭐

- **arXiv:** 2505.21552 (May 2025)
- **Author:** Diogo Cruz (Pivotal)
- **What it does:** Direct follow-up to Jenner et al. (2024). Extends the look-ahead analysis from move 3 ahead to moves 5 and 7. Three new findings:
  1. Look-ahead behavior is **highly position-dependent** — capture vs. mate-in-N positions store and process future-move information differently.
  2. The network considers **multiple move sequences simultaneously**, not just the principal variation. Corrupting squares relevant to one line redirects the model to an alternate line, as predicted by true look-ahead.
  3. Specific attention heads respond preferentially to longer-term future moves; near-term and long-term planning use distinct but consistent internal mechanisms.
- **Why it matters for this project:**
  - **Phase 6:** Settles a design question for the `did_you_consider` handler — the network *does* hold multiple lines simultaneously, so extracting alternate-line activations is a real signal, not an artifact. Build the differential extractor with this in mind.
  - **Phase 7:** Empirical support for the Fudan reasoning-pathway framing (multiple paths, not a single line) but from an independent methodology. Mutually reinforcing evidence.
- **Important model-variant note:** Cruz uses Jenner et al.'s *finetuned, history-stripped* Leela (single-board-state input rather than the 8-position history that standard Lc0 takes). This is **not the BT4 network you pinned** in ADR-0001. The methodology transfers; specific quantitative results do not. If you ever want to replicate his exact experiments, plan for a history-stripping finetune or a port of his patching code to BT4. CLAUDE.md §8 "Reconstructing positions … without preserving FEN metadata" is adjacent to this concern.
- **Feasibility signal:** Cruz reports the whole study ran on an **RTX 3070Ti in 2 days total**. That is a meaningful data point for your cost ceiling — Phase 6 activation patching does not require A100 time on Secure Cloud, and the Community Cloud 4090 is more than enough for analogous work.

#### Davis & Sukthankar — "Decoding Chess Mastery: A Mechanistic Analysis of a Chess Language Transformer Model"

- **Authors:** Austin L. Davis, Gita Sukthankar (UCF) — the *same authors* as the Davis MDPI paper already in PRIOR_WORK.md
- **Subject model:** Toshniwal et al.'s Learning Chess Blindfolded (LCB), a GPT-2-small (12L × 12H) trained on UCI move sequences. **Not Lc0.**
- **What it does:** Applies Direct Logit Attribution (DLA) per layer; finds early layers already select moves that the final layer ranks highly. Introduces an attention-pattern taxonomy for chess models.
- **Why it matters for this project:** Same family of insight as the "Iterative Inference in Chess Networks" paper already on your Phase 1 reading list — early layers contain usable signal, the network's "mind" changes meaningfully across depth. The DLA approach is the natural extractor for your `policy_layer_trajectory` handler concept.
- **Where it fits:**
  - **Phase 2** (or late Phase 1): Methodological reference for an extractor that watches the policy distribution evolve across BT4's 15 layers.
  - **Phase 3:** Their attention-pattern taxonomy is a useful priors source when designing concept probes — though it was developed on GPT-2 chess, not BT4 with smolgen, so the specific patterns won't transfer.
- **Caveats:** Smaller, weaker model than Lc0. Don't over-index on the specific findings (LCB plays maybe 1800 Elo at best); take the *methodology* and re-derive on BT4. Same caveat the Davis MDPI entry in PRIOR_WORK.md already flags.
- **Relationship to the existing Davis MDPI entry:** This is the *earlier* paper from the same authors. The MDPI 2026 paper extends this with explicit linear-probe-vs-SAE comparisons. If you're picking one to read deeply, the MDPI paper is still the priority; this one is useful background.

### Under "Research — Concept probing methodology"

#### Lovering, Forde et al. — "Evaluation Beyond Task Performance: Analyzing Concepts in AlphaZero in Hex" ⭐

- **Venue:** NeurIPS 2022
- **Authors:** Charles Lovering, Jessica Zosa Forde, George Konidaris, Ellie Pavlick, Michael L. Littman (Brown)
- **What it does:** Adapts two NLP evaluation techniques — probing classifiers *and* behavioral tests — to AlphaZero on Hex. Probes for nine known Hex concepts (bridge, edge templates, ladders, dead cells, etc.) and additionally runs **behavioral tests**: scenarios where the *only* way to play correctly is to recognize a specific concept.
- **Three findings that directly affect your project:**
  1. **"MCTS discovers concepts before the neural network learns to encode them."** Per-phase implication: relying on network-only probes (Phase 3) will *systematically* miss signal that MCTS already has. This is empirical justification for Phase 2 (MCTS integration) sitting between Phase 1 (network-first) and Phase 3 (probes) in the phase order — your sequencing was already right, but now there's a citation behind it.
  2. **Short-term end-game concepts are best represented in *final* layers; long-term concepts are best represented in *middle* layers.** This affects probe placement in Phase 3 — don't probe one layer for "everything," probe layer ranges per concept class.
  3. **"Probing performance alone is insufficient: information may be encoded but not used."** They cite this as the motivation for adding behavioral tests. **This should join the Phase 3 mandatory reading list** alongside Hewitt & Liang's control-tasks paper — same family of methodological refinement.
- **Why it matters for this project:** Phase 3 already follows the McGrath AlphaZero template (probe for human concepts). This paper adds the *behavioral-test* layer on top of probing, which closes the "is the model actually using this concept?" gap that pure probes leave open. The Phase 3 exit criterion in CLAUDE.md §11 — "70+ probes pass AUC>0.9" — measures encoding, not use. Behavioral tests measure use. **Worth proposing for an addition to Phase 3 exit criteria once the probe set is in place.**
- **Where it fits:**
  - **Phase 2:** "MCTS discovers concepts first" is a direct argument for the `compare_to_stockfish` and MCTS-tree-evaluation handlers — engine signal carries information network probes won't recover.
  - **Phase 3:** Required reading. Behavioral-test methodology should inform handler design.
  - **Phase 8:** Their eval-design philosophy ("evaluation beyond task performance") maps to your eval framework. The eval grader concept (CLAUDE.md §11 / PHASE_NOTES.md Chunk 7) can adopt their distinction between encoding metrics and behavioral metrics.

## Per-paper confirmation notes for the 6 already-referenced items

These don't need new entries — the existing PRIOR_WORK.md treatment holds. Notes below are refinements to fold into the existing entries.

**McGrath et al. — Acquisition of Chess Knowledge in AlphaZero.** Existing entry is accurate. One detail worth highlighting in Phase 3 prep: the paper explicitly draws its concept list from *Stockfish's evaluation features* — that source is the obvious starting point for your own 70–100 concept list, with chess-engine evaluation terms already mapped to scalar measurements. Grandmaster Kramnik's qualitative analysis section is also a model for what your "strong-player friends review" step at Phase 1 exit should produce.

**Jenner et al. — Evidence of Learned Look-Ahead.** Existing entry holds. The headline number is 92% probe accuracy on move-2-ahead prediction *in board states where Leela finds a single best line* — that conditioning matters and isn't captured in the current PRIOR_WORK summary. Cruz's follow-up (above) is the natural pair-reading.

**Schut et al. — Bridging the Human–AI Knowledge Gap.** Existing entry is correct but undersells the Phase 5 relevance. The methodology — identify a candidate concept, prototype positions, have 4 GMs study and improve on them — is a direct template for your Phase 5 feature labeling validation step. Suggest promoting to a Phase 5 ⭐.

**Poupart — Contrastive Sparse Autoencoders.** Existing entry is correct (⭐ for Phase 4). The framing detail worth surfacing: he trains CSAEs on **pairs of game trajectories** rather than single positions, specifically to interpret *planning* (multi-step reasoning). That paired-trajectory framing maps directly onto your Phase 6 differential analysis — diff (position, position-after-user's-move) is structurally the same as his (trajectory A, trajectory B). Worth adding a cross-reference from Phase 6.

**Karvonen et al. — Measuring Progress in Dictionary Learning.** Existing entry holds. Specific detail to surface in Phase 4 reading: they introduce **p-annealing** as a new SAE training technique (improves both unsupervised and their new supervised SAE metrics). If you end up training your own SAEs (Fudan no-go), p-annealing is a methodology choice to evaluate.

**Gao, Tour, Tillman et al. (OpenAI) — Scaling and evaluating sparse autoencoders.** Existing entry holds. Specific detail to surface in Phase 4: their **k-sparse SAE** approach simplifies training (direct sparsity control, no L1 tuning) and they release evaluation metrics that map to feature quality. If you build your own Phase 4 SAE training pipeline, k-sparse is the simpler baseline; JumpReLU (already cited) is the current SOTA refinement.

## Open questions surfaced by this reading

These are not action items — they're tracking items to drop into PHASE_NOTES.md "Open questions for the project."

1. **History-stripped Leela variant.** Cruz and Jenner both work with a single-board-state finetune of Leela rather than the 8-position-history input BT4 takes. Phase 6 activation patching may need either (a) a similar history-stripping finetune of BT4, or (b) careful experimental design that controls for history-plane noise. Worth raising before designing Phase 6 patching experiments.
2. **Behavioral tests in eval grader.** Lovering et al.'s behavioral-test methodology should be considered for the eval grader in PHASE_NOTES.md Chunk 7. Probing-only metrics measure encoding; behavioral metrics measure use. The Phase 3 exit criterion currently uses AUC>0.9 (encoding); a complementary "concept used correctly in N positions" behavioral metric might be worth adding before Phase 3 exit.
3. **MCTS-vs-network signal split.** Lovering finding that MCTS discovers concepts first formalizes a Phase 2 design question: when handlers cite "Leela sees X," does X come from the policy network or from MCTS visits/values? Worth distinguishing in the JSON schema so the LLM translator can be precise ("the network's policy says…" vs "after 5000 nodes of MCTS…").
4. **Two more attention head taxonomies.** Davis & Sukthankar (chess GPT-2) and Cruz (Leela history-stripped) both produce attention-head taxonomies. Neither targets BT4 with smolgen. Phase 3 or Phase 6 may benefit from deriving a BT4-specific taxonomy; cite both as priors.
5. **Maia-2 vs Maia-1 in your project.** PRIOR_WORK.md currently lists Maia-1100, 1300, etc. (the Maia-1 ensemble). Maia-2 (Tang et al., 2024) is the *unified skill-aware* successor and is what the Skill Adaptation paper uses. Worth deciding which to integrate when Phase 6 differential analysis needs a "how would a player rated X see this?" reference model.
